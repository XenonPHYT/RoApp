1. Introduction and scope

1.1

This Privacy Policy explains what personal information RoApp handles, why, who can see it, how long it is kept and what choices you have. It applies to the RoApp website and dashboard and to RoApp’s Discord application (commands, panels and private messages). It is a separate document from the Terms of Use, and words defined there (such as Community, Applicant, Reviewer and Service) mean the same here.

1.2

RoApp is a multi-community platform. Some information is needed to run the platform itself, and some belongs to a Community’s recruitment process. This policy keeps those apart (section 3) and explains who is responsible for what (section 2).

1.3

This policy does not cover Discord, Roblox or Cloudflare, which have their own privacy notices, or the way an individual Community uses information outside RoApp.

1.4

We have written this policy with common privacy principles in mind, including those of the Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012, the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and California privacy laws. Which laws apply depends on who you are, where you are and what processing is involved. We do not claim that RoApp is subject to, or compliant with, every provision of every law, and we have not obtained any certification or registration. Where a law gives you mandatory rights, those rights apply in addition to this policy.

1.5

We have not yet determined which laws apply to each activity. [legal review required]

3. Information we handle

3.1

The information falls into three groups.

3.2

A. Platform operational information (needed to sign you in, protect the platform and keep it running):

  • your Discord user ID and the display name or username Discord gives us at sign-in (kept while your session lasts);
  • your Discord access token, stored encrypted, which lets the dashboard ask Discord which servers you belong to (we do not store a refresh token);
  • session records: a hashed session identifier, a CSRF token, the community you selected, and when the session was created and expires;
  • a one-time sign-in record (a hash, valid for about 10 minutes) and a matching cookie, used to stop forged sign-ins;
  • short-lived counters that limit repeated requests, keyed by a SHA-256 hash of your network address for sign-in attempts, and by community and user ID for other actions. A hash can still be personal information because the original may be recoverable;
  • a record of each Discord interaction ID (for about 15 minutes) to avoid processing the same click twice;
  • generic error categories in application logs, which by design contain no answers, tokens, cookies or message text.
3.3

B. Community recruitment information (belongs to a Community’s recruitment process and is held by RoApp for it):

  • the Community’s Discord server ID, name and owner’s ID, its settings, and which Discord roles have dashboard access and with which permissions;
  • forms and panels the Community creates, including the channel and message IDs of panels;
  • Applications: the Applicant’s Discord user ID, the answers (which may include a Roblox username and anything else the Community’s questions invite), the form version, status, the public reason for a decision, and timestamps;
  • earlier answer sets, kept when an Applicant revises an application at a Community’s request;
  • unfinished answers saved as you go (drafts);
  • reviewer assignments (Reviewer Discord IDs), internal notes (author ID and text), and the history of status changes;
  • audit records: who did what, to which record, and when;
  • records of private messages sent about application status (recipient ID, the message text, delivery state and a generic error category).
3.4

C. Third-party information is information handled by Discord, Cloudflare and Roblox when they provide the services RoApp relies on (section 16). We do not control how they handle it.

3.5

What we do not collect: the sign-in does not request your email address, and RoApp does not ask for phone numbers, payment details or passwords. The website uses no advertising, analytics or social-media trackers. However, Applicants can type anything into free-text answers, so Communities decide what is asked and received; Communities should not ask for sensitive information (see the Terms of Use).

3.6

Our hosting provider may process technical request information, such as network addresses, as part of delivering the Service. Which of that is logged or kept is [operator to confirm hosting log, analytics and log-export settings].

4. Where the information comes from

4.1

  • From Discord: your user ID and name at sign-in; the list of servers you belong to; and, when the Service checks access or registers a Community, the server’s name and owner and your roles in it. 6. Discord sign-in and account authentication

6.1

When you choose “Sign in with Discord”, we ask Discord for two permissions (scopes): identify, which gives your user ID and name, and guilds, which lists the servers you belong to. We do not request your email address. The sign-in does not allow RoApp to read your messages or to act as you.

6.2

To protect the sign-in from forgery, each attempt creates a single-use random value that is stored as a hash for about 10 minutes and matched to a cookie in your browser.

6.3

After sign-in we store your Discord access token encrypted (AES-GCM, with a key held outside the database). It is used to ask Discord which servers you belong to, and it is deleted when your session ends or expires. We do not store a refresh token.

6.4

You can withdraw RoApp’s authorisation in your Discord settings at any time. If you have signed in before, the next sign-in may skip Discord’s approval screen until you sign out or withdraw access.

6.5

Separately from sign-in, RoApp’s bot works in a Community with the permissions the Community Owner granted it in Discord.

7. Dashboard sessions and authentication records

7.1

A session ends after a period of inactivity (the Operator’s setting; the default is 8 hours) and is extended while you use the dashboard, but never beyond a maximum from sign-in (the default is 7 days). Signing out ends the session at once. [operator to confirm the production settings]

7.2

The session identifier is stored only as a hash. Your community permissions are checked with Discord on each request and are not stored in the session. Signing in again replaces the earlier session of that browser, and expired sessions, and stale or invalid session cookies, are removed.

7.3

A CSRF token is stored with the session to protect actions you take in the dashboard.

8. Community and server information

8.1

When a Community Owner registers a Discord server, RoApp stores the server’s ID, name and owner ID, which are provided by Discord, and the owner’s settings. Community Owners can give dashboard access to Discord roles; the role IDs and permissions chosen are stored.

8.2

When Community Staff publish a panel, the bot posts a message in the channel they choose, and the channel and message IDs are stored so the message can be updated or removed.

8.3

Removing RoApp from a Discord server does not delete this information (see section 18).

9. Application forms and applicant submissions

9.1

A Community decides what a form asks. Your answers, the version of the form you answered, your Discord user ID, your application’s status and any public reason for a decision are stored and are visible to you and to the Community Staff who have permission to review applications.

9.2

While you complete a multi-step application, your answers so far are saved as a draft so you can continue. A draft is removed after 24 hours without activity, when you submit, or when you start over. If the form is republished in the meantime, the draft is no longer used.

9.3

If a Community asks you to revise an application, the earlier answers are kept in the application’s history. Withdrawing an application changes its status; it does not delete the answers.

9.4

When an application changes status, the Service can send you a private Discord message containing the reference, the new status and any public reason. The record of that message (recipient ID, text, delivery state) is kept.

9.5

Communities should tell Applicants how they will use the information. Please avoid including sensitive personal information or information about other people in your answers.

10. Roblox usernames and related identifiers

10.1

If a form asks for a Roblox username, it is stored as part of your application. When you or a Reviewer with permission requests a lookup, we send only that username to Roblox’s public username service and receive the public account ID and name, which are then stored with the application.

10.2

The lookup shows that a public Roblox account with that name exists. RoApp does not verify that you own it. Roblox’s own privacy notice applies to the lookup on its side.

11. Reviewer assignments, decisions and private notes

11.2

11.3

12. Audit history and activity records

12.1

For accountability, the Service records who took an action, which record it concerned and when (for example that an application was submitted, a decision was made or a setting changed). Status changes also record the reason given. These records do not include network addresses.

12.2

Community Staff with the audit permission can view the audit history of their Community. The records cannot be edited in the dashboard.

15. Access permissions and disclosures

15.1

Who can see information inside RoApp:

  • You: your own applications, their status and the public reasons.
  • Community Staff: only for their own Community and only with the matching permission (for example reviewing applications, adding notes, deciding, viewing the audit history, managing forms, panels, settings or staff). The Community Owner has all permissions.
  • Other Communities: no access. Information is separated by Community and access is checked against Discord on each request.
15.2

RoApp’s intended operating model keeps platform administration separate from Communities’ recruitment decisions. The application has no screen or feature that lets platform staff browse application answers or reviewer notes, and no platform-wide override.

15.3

We cannot promise that no one at the Operator can ever technically reach this information. Application answers and notes are stored in the platform’s database without application-level encryption, so a person who administers the database or hosting account could read them, for example when exporting or repairing data. The Operator’s policy is to limit that access to what is necessary to run, secure and repair the Service and to meet legal requirements, and not to read recruitment records otherwise. 20. Security measures and incident handling

20.1

Measures built into the Service include:

  • encrypted storage of your Discord access token, and session identifiers stored only as hashes;
  • HTTPS-only, HttpOnly and SameSite cookies, CSRF checks and origin checks on actions that change data;
  • checks against Discord, on the server, for every Community-specific request, and separation of each Community’s data;
  • verification of the signature on every request that claims to come from Discord;
  • validation of inputs, parameterised database queries, rate limits and security headers; and
  • no use of third-party scripts on the website.
20.2

Limits: application answers and notes are not encrypted at application level (see section 15), and the Service has not been independently audited or certified. No system is perfectly secure, and we do not guarantee that information will never be accessed without authorisation.

20.3

If we become aware of a breach affecting personal information, we intend to contain it, assess the risk, and notify affected people, Communities and authorities where the law requires and within the time it requires. No written incident-response procedure has yet been confirmed. [operator to confirm incident procedure and notification responsibilities]
